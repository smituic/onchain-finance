## Payment Integrity — CallData Binding (narrow hardening, not a numbered milestone; CLOSED at `bfc732974ce40fed256c2598c00041fe62da6385`; NOT deployable)

ARCHITECTURE.md's "Payment Integrity — CallData Binding" is the record; this is the operating summary. M3 and M3D statuses are unchanged (M3D OPEN); nothing is renumbered; this is not named M3D-3.

- **Status, kept distinct:** the Builder reproduced the exploit offline, fixed it, and an independent audit (Opus 5.5, High, read-only and source-only — it ran no code) returned PASS WITH FOLLOW-UP RISKS, ready for documentation closeout. The documentation audit then passed and the operator's manual Git checkpoint is `bfc7329` ("fix: bind Pay calldata to reserved payment intent", verified against `git log`): the slice is formally CLOSED, with HEAD, `origin/main` and `origin/HEAD` synchronized and the tree clean. Closure is not a deployment: don't call it deployed or production-ready, and don't say all payment-integrity risks are resolved.
- **What it fixed:** viem spreads the raw paymaster stub/final responses over the prepared operation, so a response containing `callData` replaced the locally encoded transfer and could reach `awaiting_authorization` (observed offline, including a Safe `DELEGATECALL`). `pimlico.ts` now encodes the Cash transfer locally (`account.encodeCalls`, before any provider call), compares the prepared `callData` with it by exact string equality, throws `PreparedCallDataMismatchError` on any difference, and returns the local encoding. `payments.ts` requires the reserved amount to equal the validated amount and passes the reserved amount on; the recipient already came from the reservation. A mismatch uses the existing `prepared → failed` / `prepare_failed` path: no new state, transition, provider call or migration.
- **Rules for future work:**
  - Never treat paymaster-, bundler- or other provider-returned `callData` as authorization authority. The only trusted callData is the one encoded locally.
  - Derive payment intent (recipient, amount, token, target, value, operation) from durable server-side state — the reservation — never from the request body again, the browser or a provider response.
  - Preserve exact economic-action binding and keep the comparison strict. Don't loosen it to a prefix, a decoded-equivalence or case-insensitive check, and don't add a "normalize and accept" path.
  - Don't weaken the fail-closed behavior: a refused preparation fails the attempt before `awaiting_authorization`, carries no provider text, and is never retried automatically.
  - Keep every existing Safe and signing invariant: Safe is the account; Turnkey is signer infrastructure; the handle pays the Safe, never the owner address; no blind resend; no network inside DB transactions.
  - Treat gas/fee ceilings and paymaster policy as a **separate** trust boundary (nonce, Safe factory fields and structural sponsorship are covered by L-1a, below). Don't invent gas caps or a paymaster allow-list; they need research and their own scoped slice.
- **Open, separately scoped (historical at closure; L-1a below has since added structural checks for the nonce, factory fields, sponsorship and numeric shape):** **L-1** — at closure the nonce, factory, factoryData, gas limits, fees and paymaster fields were provider-supplied and unvalidated; gas/fee ceilings and paymaster identity remain open after L-1a; conditional exposure may exist if sponsorship is removed or replaced and Safe-held ETH can pay for gas (source-inferred, not tested). **L-2** — after `prepareUserOperation`, hashing and patch construction run outside the `try/catch`, so a malformed provider field could strand an attempt in the cancellable `prepared` state (pre-existing; availability-related). Also: no submit-time callData decode and no browser decode before signing; attempts prepared before the fix are not retroactively validated; the encoder (permissionless `encodeCalls`) is a trust assumption; error observability is thin (and must never log hostile provider text); golden encoding vectors are optional future hardening.
